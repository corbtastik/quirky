---
title: "On learning to say no, thirty years late"
date: 2023-04-19
description: Saying yes to everything is a decision about your time that you have let other people make.
tags: [behavior-change, work]
---

For most of my twenties and all of my thirties I said yes by default. I thought
of this as being helpful. It was closer to being unable to tolerate the two
seconds of discomfort that a no costs.

## The reframe that worked

Every yes is a no to something. That is obvious written down and was invisible
to me in the moment, because the thing being declined was never in the room -
it was next week's version of me, who had no vote.

What finally helped was making that person present: before agreeing to
anything, look at the week it lands in and ask what gets dropped. Not
"can I do this" - something always can be done - but "what am I choosing
against".

## What I say now

"I can't take that on properly, so I'd rather not take it on." The *properly*
is doing the work. It is not a judgement about the request, and it closes the
negotiation about whether I could squeeze it in, because that was never the
question.
