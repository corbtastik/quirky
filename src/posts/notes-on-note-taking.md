Every note-taking system I have abandoned was abandoned for the same reason: it
asked me to decide, at the moment of writing, where a note belonged.

That decision is the expensive part. It is also the part I am worst at, because
at the moment of writing I do not yet know what the note is about. I find that
out later, usually by needing it for something unrelated.

## So: time first, topics later

The [stream](/) is append-only and sorted by when. There is no decision to make
when adding to it, which means I actually add to it.

The [tags](/tags/) are the same notes re-sorted by topic. A note can carry
several. They are derived, not authored - which means I can change my mind
about what something was about without rewriting anything.

## The part that surprised me

The tags are useful far less often than I expected, and the stream far more.
Most of the time I am not looking for "everything about X" - I am looking for
the thing I was thinking about around the time of some other thing I remember
better. Chronology turns out to be a remarkably good index for a human memory.

## Rules I actually keep

- One note, one idea. If it needs two headings it is probably two notes.
- Write the description first. If I cannot write one line about why this matters,
  the note is not ready.
- Never delete. Revise in place and let the old version stay in the history.
