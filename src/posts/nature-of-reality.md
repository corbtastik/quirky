This started as a question I could not answer about myself, which is usually
the sign that a question is worth writing down.

## Step one: where does meaning come from?

Pick the one that makes you least uncomfortable.

1. **It is built in.** Meaning exists whether or not anyone is around to
   notice it. The universe has a grain, and you can run with it or against it.
2. **We make it.** Meaning is something people construct, together, out of
   nothing in particular. It is real in the way money is real.
3. **It is a category error.** Asking what things mean is like asking what
   colour Tuesday is - a question the universe has no machinery to answer.

## Step two: what happens to it?

Then pick again.

1. **It accumulates.** Things are, slowly and unevenly, getting better, and
   what you add is kept.
2. **It cycles.** The same patterns come around again at different scales.
   Nothing is permanent and nothing is new.
3. **It disperses.** Everything tends toward the uniform and the cold, and
   meaning is a local, temporary eddy in that.

## What the pairs say

Nine combinations. Most people I have asked are a *2/1* - meaning is
constructed, and it builds up. That is the default modern answer and it is
load-bearing for a lot of other beliefs, which is exactly why it is worth
noticing that you hold it.

I am a *2/3*, which sounds bleak written down and does not feel bleak at all
from the inside. If meaning is something we make and it does not last, then
making it is the whole job rather than a step toward something else.

The point of the exercise is not the answer. It is that most of us have never
said the answer out loud, and then are surprised to find how many of our
smaller opinions were downstream of it. Mine are scattered through the
[stream](/), where I can check them against what I actually do.
